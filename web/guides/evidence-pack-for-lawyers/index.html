What an evidence pack is

A Studio evidence pack is a ZIP built from the results on screen: report.html (a readable report), captures.csv / .json / .jsonl (every capture with its timestamp and snapshot link), manifest.json and manifest.sha256 (a hash of every file, so any later change is detectable), and a README.txt. Studio includes 10 evidence-pack reports; each one you build is logged on the dashboard (used X / 10), and the 11th is blocked with a top-up prompt. Packs you save are stored for 12 months (free: 30 days), with a 30-day grace period after a refund, and deleted immediately on request.

Triage, not court-ready. This pack is a preservation lead: it locates the capture and preserves a dated, citable record of it. It carries no qualified timestamp, no vendor affidavit, and no chain-of-custody statement. Before relying on anything in a filing, verify every snapshot yourself at web.archive.org and have counsel confirm what your jurisdiction requires. Dedicated capture tools (Page Vault, from $199 per capture; Hunchly, $169/yr) exist precisely for the defensible-capture half of the job.

Building a pack, step by step

  1. Search the handle on the console — one network at a time, with a date range covering the matter. Pro covers all 14 networks and 2,500 captures per search; Studio raises that to 25,000 and 200 batch targets.
  2. Run the deleted diff (Research tools → Deleted diff). Each post is checked live and marked live, deleted, or unknown. Unknown means the live site was login-walled or errored — it is never counted as deleted, and the pack says so.
  3. Build citations first. Research tools → Citations turns the results into a list with the archived URL, Wayback timestamp, snapshot link, live verdict, and retrieval date — the citation format, in copy-pasteable text or .md/.csv.
  4. Build the evidence pack (Studio). The pack is built from the rows on screen, so run the search and the diff before you click it. Post text is not fetched into the pack in the browser — the pack preserves the capture record and the links, and the report says so.
  5. Verify the snapshots. Open each replay link yourself. A capture that replays as a blank shell still proves the URL was captured on that date, but it does not prove the content — say which is which in your notes.

What the pack proves — and what it doesn't

  • Proves: a public post at a URL was captured by the archive on a date, and the capture's digest matches the manifest. Every claim in the pack is dated and linked.
  • Does not prove: who wrote the post (same handle ≠ same person), why it was deleted, or what anyone intended. CDX lists captures, not authorship.
  • Does not cover: private accounts, DMs, or anything behind a login — the archive never captured them, and this tool never asks for access. Stories are almost never archived.
  • Never use for: employment, tenant, credit or insurance decisions. This is not a consumer report under the FCRA, and archive results must never be used as one. See Trust & safety.
