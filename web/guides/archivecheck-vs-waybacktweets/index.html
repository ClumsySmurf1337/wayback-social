waybacktweets — the original, and our starting point

waybacktweets (by claromes, v1.0, May 2025) does exactly the CDX job for X: one query, date filter, HTML/CSV/JSON export, plus a Streamlit web app. It is free and open source, and this product started as a generalisation of it — the credit is on the site and in the repo. Its limit is scope: X only, one handle at a time, a Python install and a Streamlit prototype, no batch across networks, no deleted-only diff, no paid support. If your job is one X handle and you are comfortable with a Python tool, it is the right tool and it costs nothing.

Deleted Tweet Finder — the friendly one

Henk van Ess's Deleted Tweet Finder searches several archives in one place and is built for non-technical users — waybacktweets is one of its search options. Search and preview are free; unlocking a record costs 1 credit and needs a sign-in. Its limit is the same as waybacktweets': X only, no batch, no export story, and the per-record credit model gets expensive the moment you need more than a handful of posts.

ArchiveCheck — the wedge

No competitor does multi-network Wayback search + batch + export + a web UI for a one-time fee. The wedge is four things: one handle, 14 networks, one run; deleted-only diff across networks; batch + evidence-pack export as the professional workflow; and no account, no login, no API key — searches stay in the browser. The free search is genuinely useful (25 captures, CSV export); the paid packs unlock the caps, the diff, and the evidence pack. What it does not do: live scraping (dead since 2023, and a suspension risk), face search, monitoring, or verdicts — those are refused by design.

All three sit on the same archive. Every tool here queries the Wayback Machine's public CDX index (Deleted Tweet Finder adds other archives). None of them owns the data, and none can promise a capture exists — archive.org goes down, captures disappear, and owners can request exclusions. The difference between the tools is workflow, not data.

Which one should you use?

  • One X handle, once, free: waybacktweets or Deleted Tweet Finder.
  • One handle, several networks: ArchiveCheck free search — tick All networks.
  • Many handles, or proof a post was deleted: ArchiveCheck Pro ($19 launch) for batch + diff; Studio ($49 launch) for evidence packs.

Honest limits

Public archived posts only. Found ≠ guilty. Not found ≠ not real.

Every tool on this page shows public posts the archive happened to capture. None of them proves who wrote a post, why it was deleted, or what anyone intended. Trust & safety
