Catfish check in 5 minutes

What this check is — and isn't

This is a pre-meeting safety check: you were given a handle, and you want to know whether the person behind it has a real, dated history — or whether the profile appeared yesterday with no past. It is not surveillance, not monitoring, and not a verdict. The tool gives you dated captures; you decide what they mean. There is no trust score and no "catfish probability" — a tool that declares someone a scammer from an archive hit is a defamation risk, and this one refuses to do it.

The 5-minute check

  1. Minute 0–1 — broad search. On the search console, type the handle, tick All networks, and search. Free tier checks 3 networks; Pro and Studio check all 14. A real person usually has captures on more than one network, spread over years. A handle with zero captures anywhere is not proof of a fake — it may simply never have been archived — but it is a reason to slow down.
  2. Minute 1–2 — read the dates. Look at the earliest capture on each network. A profile claiming five years of history whose first capture is last month is a mismatch worth noticing. Open a few snapshots: do the old bio, photos and posts read like the same person?
  3. Minute 2–3 — deleted diff. In Research tools, run Deleted diff on the handle's main network (Starter and up). Deleted posts are normal — everyone deletes things — but a profile that wiped its entire history the week it started talking to you is a pattern, not a coincidence.
  4. Minute 3–4 — the photo. The archive holds text and links, not face matching. Reverse-image-check the profile photo separately: Google Lens and TinEye are free and find the same image elsewhere on the web. If the same photo appears under three different names, stop there. Do not use face-recognition search — it is biometric surveillance, and this tool deliberately does not offer it.
  5. Minute 4–5 — decide on evidence, not vibes. If the archive shows a consistent, dated history across networks, that is reassuring — not proof, but reassuring. If it shows nothing, a brand-new profile, or contradictions, don't send money and don't meet alone. Meet in public, tell someone, and keep the conversation on the platform until you have.

What the archive can't tell you

  • Phone, email, address, dating-app membership. The archive holds public posts, not people-search records. Anyone selling you a "full background report" from an archive search is inventing it.
  • Whether two handles are the same person. The same handle on two networks proves a handle existed twice — not that one person owns both. False positives are routine.
  • Intent. A deleted post proves a post existed and was removed. It does not prove why.
  • Instagram and Facebook depth. Both are login-walled and thin in the archive — a clean result there means "not captured", not "clean person". See the Instagram guide.

If you feel unsafe, act on that first. If someone is pressuring you for money, gifts, or personal details, or the conversation feels wrong, stop — and contact a local support helpline or a romance-scam prevention resource before anything else. An archive search is a tool, not a safety plan.

It has a dating-site index and a large reverse-image database that this tool does not — but it has no archived-post timeline and no deleted-post history, and the subscription auto-renews. This check is free, one-off, and shows you the one thing they can't: what the handle posted and deleted, with dates. For the full comparison, see ArchiveCheck vs Social Catfish.

Honest limits

Public archived posts only. Found ≠ guilty. Not found ≠ not real.

Check a profile you were given, before you meet or send money. Never monitor a partner, never stalk an ex, never look up anyone under 18, and never use results to harass or confront someone. Trust & safety
