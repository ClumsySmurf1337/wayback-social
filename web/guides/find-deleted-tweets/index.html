How to find deleted tweets

Search the public archive for an X handle's deleted posts — with the date each one existed and a link to its replay. Free, no account.

What you get

A deleted tweet is gone from X, but the Wayback Machine may have captured it first. A search returns a dated list of captures: the capture timestamp, the original post URL, and a link to the archived snapshot you can open and read. A match proves the post existed on that date. It does not prove who wrote it or why it was deleted.

X has the best archive coverage of any network in this tool — the Wayback Machine has been saving X posts since 2006. That is why the guide starts here.

The search, step by step

  1. Open the search console and pick network X / Twitter.
  2. Type the handle without the @ — for example jack. One handle at a time; to check the same handle on all 14 networks, tick All networks instead.
  3. Set a date range with the From / To pickers (YYYYMMDD). A wide range is slower; a narrow range — say, the month the post vanished — is faster and cleaner.
  4. Search archive. Archive.org is slow: 20–60 seconds is normal, and it sometimes stalls or answers 503. The progress bar names the current step; Pause, Stop and Resume work, and a stalled request fails honestly after 120 seconds with a retry — it never silently shows you nothing.
  5. Read the rows. Archived is the capture timestamp. Post is the original URL. Archived snapshot opens the replay on web.archive.org. Status is the HTTP status the archive recorded.

Free tier: 25 captures per search, CSV export. Starter ($9 launch, $19 regular) raises that to 250 captures and adds JSON export; Pro ($19 launch, $29 regular) to 2,500 captures, all 14 networks, and keyword search over fetched text. All packs are one-time purchases — no subscription.

Try it first: the jack demo

Not sure what a result looks like? The console has a demo search (jack, X) button. It returns X's first post — "just setting up my twttr" — captured 21 March 2006. That is the oldest post in the archive for that handle, and the demo row is always labelled as a demo, never presented as a live result.

Which ones are actually deleted? Use the deleted diff

A capture list alone does not tell you a post is gone — it only tells you it existed. Deleted diff (in Research tools on the console, Starter and up) checks each archived post against the live site and marks it live, deleted, or unknown:

  • Deleted — the live post answers 404 or 410.
  • Live — the live post answers 200.
  • Unknown — the live site was login-walled, rate-limited, or errored. Unknown is never counted as deleted.

For X, the live check uses oEmbed — the only sanctioned no-login check X still allows. A single post loads logged-out; timelines and most profiles are login-walled, so the diff checks post URLs one by one.

What the archive can and can't do for X

  • Good: post URLs, dates, and replay text for most public posts. Deleted posts keep their snapshot link.
  • Patchy: some captures replay as a blank shell — the post text may still be in the page source, or recoverable through oEmbed. Private accounts are never captured. Most profiles and timelines are login-walled now.
  • Not real posts: sequential placeholder IDs (1, 10, 1000) answer 404 but were never posts. A 404 means "not live now", not proof a post ever existed — the diff skips listing rows with no post ID for exactly this reason.
  • Slow and flaky: archive.org goes down for ~20 minutes at a time and answers 503. A failed search is a failed search, not an empty result — retry it.

Why archives are what's left. The old free X scrapers are dead — snscrape's Twitter module broke in June 2023, Twint was archived in March 2023 — and the official X API is now pay-per-use ($0.005 per post read since February 2026, no free tier). Reading 10,000 posts costs $50, and deleted posts are gone from it entirely. The public archive is the only no-login record of what was posted and deleted.

Honest limits

Public archived posts only. Found ≠ guilty. Not found ≠ not real.

A found post does not prove who wrote it, why it was deleted, or what anyone intended. A post that isn't found may never have been archived, may have been posted under a different handle, or may simply not be saved anywhere yet. Never use results to harass, stalk, or confront anyone — and never look up anyone under 18. Trust & safety
